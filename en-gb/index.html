Three promises

At a glance

The current song, its tempo, the next song and your notes are always in view. Nothing extra competes for your attention on stage.

On time

Time is kept by an audio engine that works directly with the audio output, bypassing the user interface — so a busy device can't nudge a beat.

Quick to set up

Get tempos by listening, bring setlists over from Tempo, spreadsheets or ChordPro, and keep everything in plain files that belong to you.

Rock-solid click

The click comes from a dedicated audio engine that works directly with the device's audio output, bypassing the app's interface. It puts every beat at its exact place in the audio stream, so opening a note, scrolling the set or switching songs never nudges a beat.

Any meter. Any pulse.

10 to 400 BPM. From 1 to 16 beats per bar over 2, 4, 8 or 16, with the tempo counted in half notes, quarters, eighths or dotted quarters. Split and join beat groups — 3+2+2, 2+2+3 or any other — and the indicator follows them. Give each beat one of five levels, from strong to muted. Subdivisions up to sixteenths and sextuplets, shuffle included, six click sounds, and tap tempo with a graph of your taps.

Setlists for every gig

As many setlists as you need — one for every band and every gig. Find a song by title, drag songs into order, and switch songs without stopping the click: the new song starts on beat one. Tempo and meter are changed on a separate edit screen, so the main screen stays clean.

One set on every device

Build the set on a Mac, rehearse with an iPad on the music stand, play the gig from your iPhone. With the library in iCloud Drive, every device opens the same files.

Notes of every kind — text, photos, PDF, audio

Keep the form, the cues and the tricky spots next to the click: formatted text, PDF charts, photos of sheet music and audio with named markers. Loop a passage or slow it down to practice it. Record an idea with the microphone. Mark up any PDF or photo with a pen, a highlighter and musical stamps — the original file stays untouched, and you can export a marked-up copy as a PDF or PNG.

Let it listen. Get the tempo.

Don't know the BPM? Pick an audio file or let Primissimo listen through the microphone — to a recording or to the band in the room. A beat-tracking neural network finds the tempo right on your device, and the audio never leaves it. Halve or double the result with one tap. If the music has no steady pulse, the app tells you so instead of guessing.

Your setlists. Your files.

Setlists are plain Markdown files, not records locked in a database. Edit them in the app or in any editor that saves plain text — a word processor such as Word or Pages can damage the file. Put the library folder in iCloud Drive, and the same setlists are on your iPhone, iPad and Mac.

Import setlists from Tempo, spreadsheets and ChordPro

Open a Tempo setlist (.slist), a spreadsheet (.csv, .tsv), or ChordPro and OnSong song files (.cho, .chopro, .chordpro, .crd, .pro, .onsong), and review every song before importing. From Tempo, BPM, time signature, accents, rhythm patterns and muted beats come across; from songbooks, the lyrics and chords go into the song's notes. Anything that can't is listed before you import, not discovered on stage.

Questions

Is Primissimo really free?

Yes. There are no ads, no account, no subscription and no time limit, and nothing is sold inside the app. Paid extras with new capabilities may appear later. We don't plan to take away anything that is free today.

What makes the click more precise?

In many metronome apps, beats are timed by the same timer that drives the screen. When the device is busy with other apps, that timer can run late and the click stumbles. In Primissimo, every click is placed in the audio stream by an engine that works directly with the audio output, bypassing the user interface, so a busy device doesn't push a beat off time.

Does it work without the internet?

Yes, completely. Tempo detection runs on the device too, and your audio is never uploaded. Only sending feedback and the optional usage statistics need a connection.

Where are my setlists stored?

In Markdown files. Keep them inside the app or choose a folder — for example in iCloud Drive, so the setlists sync between devices and stay put if you delete the app. Any text editor can open them, but save changes only in one that works with plain text: a word processor such as Word or Pages can damage the file.

Can I move my setlists from Tempo?

Yes. Open a .slist file, review the song-by-song preview and import. BPM, time signature, accents, rhythm patterns and muted beats come across, and anything that can't is listed before you confirm.

Can I import a spreadsheet or ChordPro songs?

Yes. A spreadsheet saved as CSV needs a column of song titles; BPM and time signature columns are picked up too, and artist, key, duration and notes go into the song's notes. ChordPro and OnSong files bring their tempo, time signature, lyrics and chords. To build one setlist from several song files, select them all at once in the import window: the songs follow file-name order.

What data does the app collect?

Anonymous usage statistics without any identifier, which you can switch off in Settings, and the feedback you choose to send. There is no account and no advertising tracking.

Is there an Android version?

It is planned. The Google Play button will come alive when it is ready.

Does it work with a Bluetooth pedal?

Keyboard shortcuts exist, but pedals have not been tested yet, so we do not promise it. Tell us which pedal you use through the form below.
